# Research for Video Ideas

Research exists to serve two outputs: a tighter **script** and a more
convincing **storyboard**. Stop researching when those are answerable.

## When to research

- Idea needs facts, stats, names or dates (verify anything you'll say out loud).
- Human references a look ("like Apple ads", "like those finance TikToks") —
  identify what specifically defines that look (palette? type? pacing?).
- Competitor/format research: what do top videos in this niche do in L1–L2?

## Method

1. Clarify the decision the research feeds (a script claim? a style choice?).
2. Web-search in small focused queries; prefer primary sources for stats —
   a wrong stat in a video is worse than no stat.
3. Capture findings as 3–6 bullets, each with its source, in the project's
   `storyboard.md` (appendix section) or `design.md` — not scattered in chat.
4. Convert to action: a fact becomes a candidate script line; a visual
   reference becomes a named note for the design phase ("pace like X, type
   like Y").

## Visual reference notes → design

Map references onto the style contract: which THEME values would produce
that feel (colors, font stack, durations, spring settings)? If a reference
can't be expressed with an existing style, propose a new folder in
`video-design-types/` (see `video-design-types/README.md`).

## Limits

- Never let research block the pipeline — "good enough, sourced" beats
  perfect. Timebox.
- Anything the human already provided (script, idea, references) outranks
  your findings.
